## Core Thesis

> Designing how organizations learn, align, and decide.

Supporting explanation:

> I design the systems by which organizations learn, align, and decide: bringing together human insight, business context, operational realities, and technical evidence to improve decision quality.

## Core Ideas

- Projects serve as evidence.
- Ideas are the product.
- Organizations learn through systems, not isolated studies.
- Alignment is built through transparent evidence, explicit tradeoffs, and cross-functional planning.
- Better decisions follow from better evaluation and better organizational systems.
- Data alone has never improved a decision.
- Evidence becomes useful when organizations know how to interpret, challenge, and act on it.
- Evaluation shapes what organizations optimize.
- Research creates value only when organizations know how to act on it.

## Content Formatting Rules

Each major section should be mapped to the five-role system before implementation:

- Label
- Display
- Deck
- Body
- Caption

A section should not receive a new typographic treatment simply because its content is new.

## Line-Length Guidance

- Display: 10–18 characters per line
- Deck: 40–52 characters per line
- Body: 55–68 characters per line
- Captions: short and evidentiary


## Current Section Statements

### Systems

### Learn

> Strengthening organizational learning.

Supporting explanation:

> Organizations reduce uncertainty by improving how they learn.

> The strongest learning systems help organizations ask better questions, recognize meaningful signals, evaluate evidence, discover patterns, and continuously learn from the outcomes of their decisions.

### Align

> Create confidence across people, priorities, and products.

Supporting explanation:

> Alignment isn’t agreement.

> It’s creating enough confidence in the evidence, reasoning, and tradeoffs for organizations to move forward together.

> The work is making assumptions visible, interpretations explicit, and decision criteria understandable across disciplines.

### Decide

> Improve decision quality under uncertainty.

Supporting explanation:

> Better decisions rarely depend on perfect information.

> They depend on trustworthy evidence, thoughtful judgment, and organizations capable of acting with confidence.
